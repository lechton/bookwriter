Svelte Review Cards

Eight concepts an interviewer can probe and a beginner must own. Read the question, cover the answer, answer aloud, then check the code and the figure.

0 — The Render Vocabulary: scene parts, not boxes

VocabularyIndexDesign System

Use. This gallery is the drawable half of the design workflow: after CAST names the metaphor, assemble the scene from these components instead of inventing CSS. Every component is print-safe, deterministic in Chrome and Prince, and 130mm-safe. The gallery screenshots in html/_shots/ are the visual menu — look before you build.

The parts.

  • .env-dots .env-hatch .env-blueprint — environments: the background joins the metaphor
  • .console — a developer terminal, print-safe light version
  • .paper — a printed page; .paper.old is yesterday's edition
  • .bldg — an instance/building; pair with .ghost when demolished
  • .tray — a queue or inbox holding .tray-items
  • .stamp — a rotated verdict tag (.red .grn .blu)
  • .badge — a numbered circle for sequenced steps
  • .watermark — a giant faded glyph behind content
  • .ghost .strike — dead, destroyed, absent things
  • .wire .wire-tag — connectors with inline labels; .head .down .dashed
  • .zone .zone-label — a named dashed region
  • .lane .lane-label — comparison rows with color-coded semantics

The rule. Compose from this vocabulary first; reach for custom inline CSS only for gaps, and any gap used twice gets promoted into metaphors.css.

4 — Component: .console

ObjectTerminal

Use. A developer terminal window: traffic-light bar, title, mono log lines with a highlighted entry. Grounds anything that reports, logs, or inspects ($inspect, console debugging, devtools). Print-safe light version of the dark .terminal in diagrams.css.

Note. Use .console-hl on the line that changed — the highlight is where the eye should land.

5 — Component: .paper

ObjectPage

Use. A printed page or newspaper: masthead rule plus mono body. For anything printed, rendered, or published — DOM output, batched updates, editions. .paper.old (dashed, gray) is yesterday's edition: stale output, superseded values.

7 — Component: .tray

8 — Component: .stamp

DeviceVerdict

Use. A rotated tag for verdicts and emphasis: APPROVED, DEPRECATED, SURVIVED, RESET. The tilt makes it read as a physical stamp pressed onto the page — the card's verdict in one word.

Note. One stamp per scene, two at most. A third stamp turns the card into a passport page.

10 — Component: .watermark

DeviceAtmosphere

Use. A giant faded glyph behind the content: { } for scopes, λ for functions, a rune for its own card. Adds depth and personality at zero cost to clarity. Parent panel needs position:relative.

Note. Keep opacity near 0.07: visible on screen, a whisper in print. Always behind the content, never beside it.

11 — Component: .ghost

DeviceAbsence

Use. A dead, destroyed, or ignored element: dashed gray border, no shadow, washed content. Pair with .strike on its name. This is how a diagram shows what did NOT happen — the crossed-out expectation.

Note. Ghost plus lane-label is the violation genre in two lines: the wrong expectation gets a visible grave.

12 — Component: .wire

DeviceConnector

Use. A connector with an inline label tag and an optional drawn arrowhead: every arrow in a scene is an operation, and the tag names it. .head adds the arrowhead, .down turns it vertical, .dashed makes it a dead or scheduled link. Tag variants: default black, .red (destructive), .yel (highlight), .mut (inactive).

delivers
DOM

lost
DOM

Note. The tag floats on the wire and interrupts it — the reader reads the operation while following the flow.

13 — Component: .zone

DeviceRegion

Use. A named dashed region grouping elements: retained scope, error boundary, safe area, old way. Zones answer "where does this live?" — the fog genre's best friend.

Note. The label sits on the zone's border, like a sign on a fence — the region reads as a place, not a rectangle.

14 — Component: .lane

DeviceComparison

Use. A comparison row with a color-coded semantic label: .ok green (what works), .bad red (what fails), .info blue (the new way), .mut gray (the inactive way). Lanes are the skeleton of the violation and choice genres — identical chrome, one differing fate.

Note. The label carries the rule, the boxes carry the evidence. Color-code the labels, keep the boxes neutral.
