Question. What are Svelte 5 Runes, and what specific architectural limitations of legacy reactivity do they address?

Answer. Svelte 5 Runes are explicitly declared, fine-grained reactive primitives (such as $state, $derived, and $effect). They replace the legacy component-level reactivity system to address three major architectural limitations: 1. Universal State: Legacy reactivity was strictly bound to .svelte files. Runes can be declared in raw .svelte.js or .ts files, making it effortless to share state across the entire application without using stores. 2. Explicit Deep Reactivity: Legacy Svelte relied on the assignment operator (=) to trigger updates, which caused confusion when mutating nested arrays or objects. Runes use an explicit Proxy-based signal system, so deep object mutations trigger updates automatically. 3. Fine-Grained DOM Updates: Legacy reactivity dirty-checked the entire component on every state change. Runes use a signal graph to pinpoint exact DOM nodes that depend on the state, bypassing component-level checks and vastly improving performance.

Summary. Runes untangle reactivity from the .svelte component boundary. They offer explicit, deeply reactive, and surgically fine-grained state management that scales gracefully into large codebases.
