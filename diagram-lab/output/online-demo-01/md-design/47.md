Design description: A left-to-right linear pipeline of four boxes on off-white paper, teaching the one mechanism behind `tick()`: awaiting it yields control so the deferred flush can finally run, and execution resumes with the DOM in its new state. The first box on the far left is a neutral white data container labeled `items.push('c')` with the caption "state changed, DOM stale" beneath it. A thick black arrow with a solid arrowhead connects it to the second box, which is the only mustard-yellow accent in the diagram and is labeled `await tick()`; a small pill positioned above the arrow reads "yields control", marking the moment the handler pauses and lets the microtask queue run. The second box connects via another thick black arrow to a third neutral white box labeled "flush runs" with the caption "DOM updates" beneath it; and the third connects to a fourth neutral white box labeled "execution resumes" with the caption "DOM is current" beneath it. Exactly four visual elements total: the four boxes in sequence, plus the "yields control" pill as a small annotation that does not count separately. No timeline row, no $effect comparison, no callback alternative — the diagram argues synchronization with a single yield point in a chain, nothing else.
