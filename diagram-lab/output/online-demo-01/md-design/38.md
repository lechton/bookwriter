Design description: A side-by-side comparison of two cards on off-white paper, teaching the single rule that decides between `$derived` and `$derived.by`: whether the work fits in one expression or needs statements. The left card is labeled `$derived(expr)` and shows a single thin slot meant to hold one expression, captioned "one expression" beneath it. The right card is labeled `$derived.by(() => { ... })` and shows a taller stacked block of three short statement lines (representing a loop, an intermediate `let`, and a return), captioned "statements allowed" beneath it. Both cards share identical thick black outlines and hard offset shadows, and a single horizontal mustard-yellow banner spans above both reading "same memoized signal underneath", marking the one fact the two forms share and making the banner the only mustard-yellow accent in the diagram. Exactly four visual elements total: the left card, the right card, the banner, and the two captions counted as one set. No arrows, no flowchart, no second input branch, no legacy `$:` comparison — the diagram argues its point by what each card can hold, nothing else.
