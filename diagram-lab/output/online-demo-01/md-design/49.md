Design description: A side-by-side comparison of two cards on off-white paper, teaching the single rule that decides between the two prop-sync patterns: whether the local variable must track later changes to the prop or only its initial value. The left card is labeled `$state(prop.value)` and shows a small timeline of two horizontal slots stacked vertically — a top slot marked "initial" filled with the prop's first value, and a bottom slot marked "later" shown empty with a small dashed outline, visually communicating that the local copy captured the value once and stops listening; a caption beneath reads "copied once, does not track later changes". The right card is labeled `$derived(prop.value)` and shows the same two slots but with both filled and connected by a short solid arrow from the top slot down to the bottom slot, visually communicating continuous flow from the prop into the local variable over time; a caption beneath reads "recomputes whenever the prop changes". Both cards share identical thick black outlines and hard offset shadows; the only mustard-yellow accent in the diagram is the connecting arrow on the right card, marking it as the prescriptive choice for true synchronization. Exactly four visual elements total: the left card, the right card, the two captions counted as one set, and the connecting arrow on the right card. No $effect inset, no editability comparison, no code snippet — the diagram argues its point by showing whether value flows once or continuously, nothing else.
