Design description: The graphic teaches the single mechanism that distinguishes `$state` deep reactivity from any prior design: every nested object is silently promoted to a tracked Proxy the instant you touch it, so a leaf mutation fires a leaf-only signal without ever re-evaluating a parent. The layout is a single horizontal "access chain" running left to right across the canvas, not a comparison panel. From the left, four monospaced tokens read as the expression `user . profile . preferences . theme`, each token sitting inside its own card; between every pair of cards a labeled "PROXY HAND-OFF" pill interrupts the chain, signaling that each `.` traversal returns a fresh reactive wrapper rather than the raw value. The chain terminates on the right in a highlighted "LEAF SIGNAL" node holding `theme = 'light'`. Below the leaf, a single thin, brightly glowing arrow departs and travels horizontally off to the right, passing *under* every upstream card without touching any of them, and lands in a small isolated `<h1>` DOM node container. This arrow is the prescriptive punchline: the update skips the entire ancestor chain. Above the access chain, a thin horizontal caption reads "WRAPPED ON ACCESS, NOT ON CREATION" — the one fact that separates deep reactivity from a one-shot shallow snapshot. A small inset in the lower-left shows the legacy counter-example as a single crossed-out box (`user = user`) labeled "BULK INVALIDATE", drawn deliberately smaller and greyed to communicate that the old reassignment ritual is obsolete, not merely discouraged. The palette stays disciplined: deep charcoal background, white cards, a single cyan accent for every proxy pill and the leaf signal, and one yellow arrow for the fine-grained DOM update. No red, no green, no validation framing — the diagram argues a mechanism, it does not grade an outcome.
