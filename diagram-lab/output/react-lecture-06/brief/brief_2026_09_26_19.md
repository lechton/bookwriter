# Architectural Decision Brief — 2026-09-26-19: Operational Principle-Map Authority, Cumulative Non-Overlap, and Evidence-Based Negative Guidance

## Executive Summary
This batch creates `map-principles` as the single workflow for turning `01-01-map/{nn}.md` into an approved operational contract at `01-01-map-principles/{nn}.md`. It audits Q01 through Q04 and adds Q05 and Q06 for the latest `v6` lecture set. It also replaces adjacent-only overlap checks with cumulative ownership across every previous principle map and removes the downstream rules that independently rediscovered principle counts or forced every principle into a positive/negative pair.

## 1. Root Cause Analysis

### 1.1 Principle Discovery Happened Too Late
`pre-lecture`, `lecture-structure`, and `principled-theory` each asked authors to derive principle count from the curriculum map. Because no intermediate artifact owned the result, each phase could select a different set, reorder it, or import an attractive invariant from another lesson.

### 1.2 Adjacent-Only Comparison Missed Older Duplicates
The prior non-overlap rule checked adjacent lessons. A decision could therefore reappear two or more lessons later under new wording. Q06 exposed the defect: using curly braces for dynamic JSX attributes belongs to Q04 even though props make the later example look different.

### 1.3 Mandatory Comparison Pairs Invented Prohibitions
The lecture rules required every principle to contain a positive and negative code card. That structure encouraged false bans, such as rejecting a ternary that returns `null`, even when both alternatives were valid. The problem came from the instruction architecture, not from insufficient negative wording.

## 2. Resolution (#2026_09_26_19_group_1)

### 2.1 One Principle-Selection Authority
`map-principles` now owns principle discovery, count, order, cumulative overlap review, review language, and required lesson proof. Downstream skills consume the approved map one-to-one instead of rediscovering the principle set.

### 2.2 Cumulative Operational Ownership
Every candidate is compared with all earlier principle maps. The first formal lesson owns the operational decision. Later lessons may reuse the knowledge as an unnumbered prerequisite and may create a new principle only when the mechanism, condition, developer action, or prevented failure is genuinely new.

### 2.3 Evidence-Based Review Language
Principle maps select among `Do`, `Prefer`, `Avoid`, `Do not`, and `Condition`. `Do not` is reserved for authentic scoped failures or harmful actions. Valid alternatives receive conditions or preferences rather than manufactured prohibitions.

### 2.4 Latest Lecture-Set Audit
Q01 through Q04 were rewritten into the approved map format. Q02 drops the out-of-scope nested-component rule and corrects the obsolete claim that React rejects every `undefined` return. Q04 keeps both terse and explicit conditional forms valid. Q05 and Q06 were added, and Q06 treats Q04 curly-brace binding as prerequisite knowledge rather than a new principle.
