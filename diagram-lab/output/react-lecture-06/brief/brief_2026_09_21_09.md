# Brief 2026_09_21_09: Full Concept Explanation Precedes Friction Cards

- **Date**: 2026-09-21
- **Tag**: `#2026_09_21_09_group_1`
- **Affected Skills**: `skills/lecture-voice/SKILL.md`, `skills/lecture-structure/SKILL.md`, `skills/pre-lecture/SKILL.md`, `skills/verification/SKILL.md`, `skills/RULE-TAGS.md`

## Context & Defect Analysis
In rule `#2026_09_21_08_group_1`, the sequence for placing `[!WILD]` friction cards was prescribed as `(1) baseline concept, (2) naive code demonstration, and (3) [!WILD] card`.

This formulation created a critical pedagogical defect: the author interpreted this sequence literally by dropping the `[!WILD]` card immediately after the naive failing code block. Consequently, the actual educational pedagogy of the theme—the underlying engine mechanics, the governing naming rules (PascalCase), the compiler dispatch between string literals and function references, and the working capitalized component—was shoved AFTER the wild card.

This caused the friction card to blurt out theoretical terms (`PascalCase`, `compiler transform`) before they were ever taught in the main prose, turning the following paragraph into an awkward, redundant echo.

## The Solution: De-Prescription and Natural Pedagogical Sequence
Under the Golden Rule of De-Prescription, we pruned the over-specified `(1) -> (2) -> (3)` sequence and replaced it with a simple, direct principle written in plain, common English:

> **Teach the full lesson before adding a friction card.**
> Never drop a `[!WILD]` card into the middle of an unfinished explanation. Explain the problem, show the broken code, teach the rule, and show the working code first. Only place the `[!WILD]` card after the reader understands both the mistake and how to fix it.

## Verification Gate
Gate 14 in `skills/verification/SKILL.md` was updated to reject any lecture where a friction card interrupts an unfinished explanation or sits between a broken snippet and the explanation that fixes it.
