# Decision Brief — 2026-09-25-02: Question-Based Standards Transformation & Tone Glossary Harmonization

## Context & Motivation
1. **Radical Transformation from Authoritarian Orders to Evaluative Questions**:
   Prior skill editions relied heavily on imperative commands, negative prohibitions ("never do this", "strictly forbidden", "you must", "do not"), and aggressive behavioral policing. As articulated in the meta-rules and the Golden Rule of De-Prescription, this style triggers model pathologies (the reflexive ban fallacy and the exclusionary fallacy) and produces brittle, adversarial rule accumulation.
   This overhaul systematically transforms all standards, principles, quality gates, and checklist items across `skills/v6.2/` into evaluative questions (`- [ ] Does ...?`, `- [ ] Is ...?`). Instead of barking instructions at the author or agent, each standard sets an objective, measurable bar on the resulting artifact.

2. **Adoption of Canonical Prompt Quality Checklist & Tone Glossary**:
   Integrated the canonical `quality-checklist.md` standards (Clarity and scope, Language, Structure, Output, Coding/agentic) into the project's meta-skill while preserving domain governance protocols (zero hard-wrapping, de-prescription, rule tagging, and verification).
   Adopted the canonical `glossary.md` authority and tone table:
   - Prohibitions: prefer "avoid" and condition statements; avoid "don't", "never", "only", "under no circumstances", "nothing", "not", "forbidden".
   - Instructions: prefer "prefer"; avoid "must", "only", "obligatory".
   - Actions: set standards on the result using question checklists; avoid "do", "do not", "not".
   Harmonized standard headings across PROPER EXAMPLE, HALF WAY EXAMPLE (showing partial attempts that look improved but fall short), and COUNTER-EXAMPLE items with descriptive title anchors instead of imperative labels.
