# Architectural Decision Brief — 2026-09-26-21: Source-Open Principle Discovery, Syllabus Ownership, and Importance Selection

## Executive Summary
This batch rebuilds the `map-principles` workflow and the rules that consume its maps. The count-last workflow of `#2026_09_26_20_group_1` removed explicit count targets, but its input was closed (the syllabus card and earlier maps only), its tests could only shrink a list, and its ownership rule gave each decision to the first lesson that mentioned it. The Q01-Q10 rescan therefore settled on a new default total and assigned several decisions to the wrong lessons. The new workflow runs two passes. Discovery searches wide and map-blind: the syllabus card, the react.dev Learn and Reference pages for the topic with their Recap, Pitfall, Deep Dive, Note, Challenges, Caveats, and Troubleshooting blocks, and checked senior engineering knowledge. Selection routes each candidate to the lesson whose syllabus card makes it a main subject, keeps the owned candidates that pass an importance test and the atomicity tests, and numbers them last. Every candidate, including rejected ones, stays visible in one candidate ledger, which also tells pre-lecture and lecture authors what to explain as support, recall, preview, or leave out.

## 1. Root Cause Analysis

### 1.1 Closed Input
Discovery read only the syllabus card, a short summary. Official pitfalls absent from the card could not enter any map: the react.dev Pitfall against defining a component inside another component had no owner, and the numeric-zero `&&` Pitfall sat in the wrong lesson.

### 1.2 Only Subtractive Tests
The merge, prerequisite, and supporting tests could only remove candidates. Nothing asked whether an important decision was missing, so the lists drifted toward the smallest tidy size: six of the ten rescanned maps ended at the same total.

### 1.3 First-Mention Ownership
"The first lesson that formally teaches an invariant owns it" let early lessons take decisions that later syllabus cards make their main subject: pure rendering in Q02 while the Q12 card's topic is purity, and the numeric-zero guard in Q04 while the Q10 card's Under the Hood and Code Experiment are about it.

### 1.4 An Unverifiable Count-Last Gate
The coverage ledger was written in final principle numbers ("becomes Principle 1") and rejected candidates were not kept, so no reviewer could confirm that numbering came last.

### 1.5 Self-Certifying Examples
The skill's PROPER and COUNTER examples cited the current maps' own verdicts (Q07, Q06, Q04, Q02), which locked decisions such as "Q02 owns pure rendering" into the rule text.

### 1.6 Downstream Pressure to Promote Syntax into Principles
`principled-theory` Section 02 required every property used in a case study to be "explicitly analyzed as a principle". That pushed syntax details and setup facts into principle lists (an inline-styles principle in the v6 Lecture 04, setup facts in the Q09 map) and contradicted the one-to-one map rule.

### 1.7 Stale and Conflicting Exemplars
`principled-theory` still described a nine-principle Lecture 4 ("Principles 1 through 9"), cited the v5 benchmark's zero-guard pair as Principles 5 and 6 although `v5/04.md` numbers them 4 and 5, carried fixed digits in about twenty exemplar headers, and called a ternary returning `null` an unnecessary pattern while the benchmark exemplar and the review-language rules treat it as valid.

## 2. Resolution

### 2.1 Group 1 (`#2026_09_26_21_group_1`): map-principles rewrite
- **Artifact Authority (01)**: the whole syllabus decides ownership; react.dev prevails on facts; conflicts are reported.
- **Discovery (02)**: map-blind and source-open; every Recap, Pitfall, Deep Dive, Note, Challenges, Caveats, and Troubleshooting block on the topic's pages; Rules of React and the React 19 upgrade guide when relevant; `general` candidates must state a checkable code case; card claims are checked against react.dev.
- **Ownership (03)**: a decision belongs to the lesson whose card makes it a main subject; the earliest lesson whose experiment needs it owns it when the syllabus is silent; `Prerequisite`, `Preview`, and `Deferred` dispositions name the owner; conflicts with earlier maps are reported.
- **Importance, Kind, Atomicity, and Proof (04)**: three importance questions; `Decision` and `Model` principles, with a `Prediction` for models; split and merge tests; proofs must fit the Authorized Scope; quoted error text is checked against React 19 with Vite; teaching order; numbering last.
- **Review Language (05)**: unchanged rules, examples de-anchored from lesson numbers.
- **Output Contract (06)**: `Sources Read`, `Candidate Ledger`, principles with Kind, Rule, Importance, Review Point or Prediction, Required Lesson Proof, and Sources, a `Senior Answer`, and an auditable Completion Gate.
- **Verification (07)**: one-to-one ledger match, a react.dev spot check, a fresh Discovery pass during audits, an ownership check, and author clarity.
- `agents/openai.yaml` and the README row and load order describe the two passes.

### 2.2 Group 2 (`#2026_09_26_21_group_2`): ledger-driven author handoff
- `pre-lecture` 01B: authors plan `Supporting` rows as theory prose, `Prerequisite` rows as one-line recalls, and `Preview` rows as at most one sentence naming the owner lesson; `Deferred` rows stay out; map gaps are reported instead of patched; each principle's Required Lesson Proof is planned.
- `lecture-structure` 06 and 12B, `verification` 10 and gates 23 and 24: one-to-one matching in order and decision, with ledger rows handled as prose, recalls, or brief owner-named mentions; count and "neighboring totals" language removed.
- `principled-theory`: case-study syntax may be grounded by a principle or by supporting explanation; the non-overlap standard became the Map Ownership Standard; `Model` principles are proved by their Prediction; Section 05 leaves discovery, ownership, and selection to `map-principles`.

### 2.3 Group 3 (`#2026_09_26_21_group_3`): exemplar de-anchoring and contradiction repair
- A skill-wide note in `principled-theory` states that exemplars write `Principle N` and that the v5 benchmark's numbering is historical; the narrower Golden Exemplar Bank note it replaces was removed.
- All fixed digits in exemplar principle headers in `principled-theory` and `lecture-structure` became `Principle N`; stale nine-principle Lecture 4 references were rewritten generically.
- The ternary-returning-null contradiction was replaced by an authentic contrast (a raw numeric count before `&&`, or `&&` versus a ternary by branch shape).
- Side fix: PROPER examples used `tabIndex={1}`, a positive tab order that accessibility guidance discourages; they now use `tabIndex={0}`.

## 3. What This Batch Does Not Change
- The Q01-Q10 maps in `01-01-map-principles/` are untouched. They still use the former format and first-mention ownership, so they are provisional until rebuilt with the new workflow. The audit found these inputs for the rebuild, listed without predicted totals: Q01 the component-based model and a proof that needs no re-render; Q02 the top-level component definition Pitfall, the `return`-line Pitfall as support, and purity as a preview of Q12; Q04 the numeric-zero guard as a preview of Q10 and the style-object exclusion naming Q83; Q05 mental models as `Model` principles; Q06 required-prop guidance after React 19 removed `propTypes`; Q08 Vite's native-ESM missing-export error text and filename-case failures on case-sensitive CI; Q09 setup facts as support, the `root.render` container-clearing caveat, and StrictMode deferred to Q20; Q10 the numeric-zero guard and JSX assigned to a variable.
- The glossary and Beyond-the-basics "4 to 6" ranges stay. They budget presentation (terms and links), not principle selection, and removing them risks bloat.
- The benchmark lecture `01-02-md-LECTURES/v5/04.md` is unchanged; the skills now treat its numbering as historical.
