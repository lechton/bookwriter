# Brief 2026-10-07 01: Plain titles and headings

## Problem
The owner found the Lecture 15 principle titles "very cryptic", for example "The Commit Changes Only What Differs, So Typed Text Stays". No instruction said how a title should read. The only guidance was the syntax `> **Principle N: Title**` and the Title Case shown in the casebook, so titles copied that habit and packed new words, abstractions, and two claims into one line.

## Decision
- `principle-definition` Section 01 gains a title rule (#2026_10_07_01_group_1): one plain sentence in sentence case, one claim, words the reader already has, no word the lesson is still teaching, no abstraction, no "so". Three proper and three counter examples carry it. Section 02 names sentence case in the syntax line. The casebook shows its seven approved titles in sentence case; failed drafts keep their original case.
- `lecture-structure` Section 10B (#2026_10_07_01_group_2) applies the same rule to the section headings a lesson writes for itself. The fixed skeleton headings and step headings keep their form.
- Lectures 14 and 15 and pre-lecture 15 follow the rule (#2026_10_07_01_group_3). Lecture 14 wording is unchanged apart from case; its first heading "A Like Button That Counts in Secret" becomes "The like count changes, but the screen does not".

## Lecture 15 titles
1. After a setter call, React runs the component and then changes the page
2. After a setter call, React runs that component and its children, not the whole app
3. Running a component again does not rebuild the page
4. The page changes only after the click handler has finished

## Not changed
- Map headings, which already use sentence case.
- No script check for sentence case; the rule and its examples carry it.
