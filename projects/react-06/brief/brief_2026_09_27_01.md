# Architectural Decision Brief — 2026-09-27-01: Calibrated Vocabulary Net, Contextual Nuance, and Multi-Sentence Operational Instruction Pedagogy

## Executive Summary
This batch updates the meta-rules governing prompt authoring (`prompt-writing/quality-checklist.md`) and the specific instructions governing principle definitions (`principled-theory/SKILL.md`). It addresses two core defects: (1) the Blanket Maximum Net Fallacy, where instructions indiscriminately mandated that technical terminology be front-loaded in every sentence, distorting conversational clarity where simple mechanics were being taught; and (2) Monolithic Instruction Syntax, where instructions themselves were authored as dense 50-to-80-word compound run-on sentences that AI models mimicked in their output. The batch introduces the Calibrated Net standard (specializing with proper specialization to cover what needs to be covered—not more, not less) and mandates that all instruction checklist items be written in multiple short, clear, operational sentences.

## 1. Root Cause Analysis

### 1.1 The Blanket Maximum Net Fallacy (Missing Nuance & Contextual Calibration)
Rule 103 previously commanded: *"Does every principle title and definition front-load and explicitly name the canonical platform and industry terminology...?"* This cast an uncalibrated, blanket maximum net across all contexts. In technical interviews, senior interviewers grade candidates on knowing exact industry vocabulary *in specific conceptual and architectural cases* (e.g. **Slot Composition**, **Inversion of Control**, **Opaque Data Structure**, **Unidirectional Data Flow**). In those specific cases, vague conversational circumlocutions that avoid standard terms are unacceptable and fail the interview. However, turning this insight into an uncalibrated global mandate forced models to inject heavy academic jargon into every opening clause, even where simple mechanical steps or junior-friendly intuition were being explained.

### 1.2 Monolithic Instruction Syntax (The Run-On Prompt Defect)
Rules 103, 104, 110, and 111 were themselves authored as dense, compound, multi-clause run-on sentences. Models naturally mirror the syntactic density, clause chaining, and tone of their instructions. When prompt instructions are dense and academic, models generate dense and academic prose. To produce clear, calm, and readable student materials, the instructions themselves must be authored in proper pedagogy using multiple short, clear, and operational sentences (averaging 10–18 words).

### 1.3 Verbatim Map Copying
Because previous instructions lacked clear division of labor regarding the map-principles artifact, models treated the legalistic `Rule` field in `01-01-map-principles/{nn}.md` as copy to be pasted directly into student-facing principle callouts, rather than translating the engineering requirement into conversational, junior-friendly prose.

## 2. Resolution

### 2.1 Prompt Writing Standards (`skills/v6.3/prompt-writing/quality-checklist.md`)
Added two foundational standards under `## Language`:
- **The Calibrated Net & Nuanced Specialization Standard**: Considers the specific circumstances and context under which every rule is framed. Mandates domain-specific sensitivity over universal blanket mandates. Requires exact industry vocabulary where senior interviewers probe it while strictly avoiding forced jargon in simple mechanics.
- **The Short Operational Instruction Standard**: Mandates that instruction checklist items themselves be authored in multiple short, clear, operational sentences (averaging 10–18 words), eliminating monolithic run-ons that models mimic.

### 2.2 Principled Theory Standards (`skills/v6.3/principled-theory/SKILL.md`)
- **Rule 103 (Calibrated Interview Vocabulary)**: Replaced the blanket "every principle must front-load" command with a calibrated net. Requires exact terminology in critical interview cases (Slot Composition, Inversion of Control, etc.) while prohibiting vague circumlocutions in those cases and banning forced jargon elsewhere.
- **Rule 104 (Operational 3-Beat Rhythm)**: Decompressed the monolithic sentence into four short, operational sentences defining Beats 1, 2, and 3.
- **Rule 110 (The Canonical Noun + Plain Human Verb Standard)**: Simplified and clarified into short, active sentences emphasizing pairing formal nouns with everyday verbs (`pass`, `receive`, `render`, `check`).
- **Rule 111 (The Natural Sequential Human Standard & Map Rule Translation)**: Decompressed into short sentences and added an explicit requirement to translate map rules into conversational human prose rather than transcribing legalistic text verbatim.
