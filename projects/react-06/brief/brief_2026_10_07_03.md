# Brief 2026-10-07 03: For the interview section and merged rows law

## Problem
Lecture 16 came out noticeably shorter than preceding chapters (~2,600 words vs ~4,500 words). The audit revealed two systemic causes:
1. Candidate ledger rows with disposition `Merged` in the principle map (such as "Is setState asynchronous?", the Alice/Bob chat message race condition, and `async/await` behavior across renders) had no designated instruction path. Authors assumed the principle box discharged them and dropped these rich proofs and debates.
2. The curriculum is titled around interview questions (`> INTERVIEW QUESTION | ❱ CORE`), but the terminal skeleton jumped straight from `Where you will meet this` into `Glossary` and `Summary`, lacking a dedicated space to deconstruct the interview dynamic, candidate failure modes, and follow-up curveballs.

## Decision
- `lecture-structure` Section 13 (#2026_10_07_03_group_1) codifies `### For the interview` as a permanent terminal skeleton section directly after `### Where you will meet this` and before `### Glossary`. It delivers four distinct beats:
  1. The Hidden Signal (What the interviewer is really testing)
  2. The Classic Trap (The common candidate wrong answer and why it fails)
  3. The 60-Second Senior Response (A crisp, authoritative model whiteboard answer)
  4. Follow-Up Curveballs (Two to three realistic follow-up questions probing edge cases and boundaries)
- `pre-lecture` Section 01B and Section 11 (#2026_10_07_03_group_1) mandate unpacking `Merged` ledger rows into theory prose, principle derivations, or `### For the interview`, preserving rich proofs and debates.
- `produce_prompt.md` updates the candidate ledger rule and skeleton order accordingly.
