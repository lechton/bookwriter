# Decision Brief — 2026-09-25-05: Flexible Assembly Steps, Professional Tutorial Example Standard, and Compiler De-Prescription

## Context & Motivation
1. **The Rigid 4-Step Choreography & Compiler Lock-in**:
   In previous iterations, Stage C was mechanically bound to exactly 4 steps (`Step 1: First...`, `Step 2: Next...`, `Step 3: Then...`, `Step 4: Finally...`). Crucially, the compiler (`src/build-lectures.mjs`) had hardcoded linting checks that issued defects if all 4 step headings were not present and if the pipeline figure did not contain all 4 pastel colors (`#d4e1f1`, `#d0e6e1`, `#f9e0c5`, `#d2ebc9`).
   This artificial constraint forced authors and AI models into a destructive pattern: regardless of the lesson topic (even simple JSX syntax or element returns), authors were forced to shred simple logic across 4 separate files (`SiteHeader.jsx`, `Child1.jsx`, `Child2.jsx`, `App.jsx`).

2. **The "Cringe" / Amateurish Example Pathology**:
   Because 4 files were mandatory, trivial markup fragments (e.g. an `<h2>` or a single `<input>` tag) were extracted into absurd standalone components. This resulted in:
   - Unnatural imports and inverted architectural boundaries that no senior frontend engineer would ever ship.
   - Contrived scenarios in The National Times newsroom invented purely to justify the 4 artificial files.
   - Loss of pedagogical focus: students spent their cognitive energy navigating file boundaries and perfunctory `App.jsx` mounts rather than mastering the core React mechanism.

3. **The Professional Tutorial Benchmark**:
   Top-tier engineering education (such as official `react.dev`, Kent C. Dodds, Josh Comeau, Dan Abramov) never forces arbitrary file shredding. A professional tutorial selects an authentic, elegant widget where the mechanism being taught is load-bearing. The assembly progression follows the natural architectural boundaries of that widget (2 to 4 organic steps, $N \in \{2, 3, 4\}$).
