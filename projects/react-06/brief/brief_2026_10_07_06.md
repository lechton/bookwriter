# Brief 2026-10-07 06: Anti-Bureaucratic Interview Standard, The Restaurant Order Model, and Plain Beat Titles

## Problem
In drafting `### For the interview` for Lectures 16 and 17, two critical pedagogical flaws emerged:
1. **Bureaucratic and Dumbed-Down Prose**: Authors defaulted to sterile spec-sheet compiler jargon ("visual layout thrashing", "receiving running pending total", "replacement instruction") rather than intuitive, physical coaching. Instead of memorable mental models, the text delivered uninspired compliance summaries that fail to stick during high-pressure whiteboard interviews.
2. **Abstract and Colloquial Beat Titles**: Beat 3 was titled "The 60-Second Senior Response", which sounded like an artificial compliance rubric rather than explaining mechanical reality. Beat 4 was titled "Follow-Up Curveballs", relying on an American sports colloquialism that violates international B2 plain-English standards and obscures the engineering content.

## Decision
- `lecture-structure` Section 13 (#2026_10_07_06_group_1) establishes the Anti-Bureaucratic Interview Standard, codifying the complete living exemplar from Lecture 17 with line-by-line commentary. It replaces Beat 3 with `**3. What is Actually Going On**` and Beat 4 with `**4. Where It Gets Tricky**`.
- Beat 3 adopts the Restaurant Order Model: React is a waiter who does not sprint to the kitchen on every dish; they wait until the user finishes ordering and hand the entire ticket to the chef once. Passing a value asks for dish number one three times, while passing an updater instructs the chef to add one more to the plate.
- Beat 4 replaces colloquial curveballs with high-stakes engineering reality: sequential queue arithmetic computing 42, asynchronous pauses (`await`) causing lost ticket increments due to stale closures, and `StrictMode` double-invocation catching external object mutations.
- `pre-lecture` Section 11, `verification` Section 18 & Gate 21, and `produce_prompt.md` (#2026_10_07_06_group_1) update the four canonical interview beat titles: (1) The Hidden Signal, (2) The Classic Trap, (3) What is Actually Going On, and (4) Where It Gets Tricky.
