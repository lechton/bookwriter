# Q139: Suspense Boundary Placement: Fast Shell vs Granular Fallbacks
* **Topic Tag**: #suspense_streaming | ❱❱ MORE (Server)
* **Interview Question**: *Where should Suspense boundaries sit: fast shell, slow holes?*
* **Core Truth**: Strategic Suspense boundary placement decouples the instant, stable layout shell from slow data holes, avoiding both monolithic page-level spinners and hyper-granular visual flickering.
* **The Friction**: The engineering team at The National Times places a <Suspense> boundary around every single article paragraph and author avatar; during streaming, 40 separate skeleton spinners flash and pop into place out-of-order, causing massive layout shifts (CLS) and disorienting readers.
* **The Mechanism**: Placement architecture: (1) Keep the root layout, navigation, and primary article body outside Suspense for an instant shell; (2) Group secondary widgets (e.g. comments, related stories, stock tickers) into cohesive, independent Suspense boundaries with matching skeleton dimensions.
* **Under the Hood**: Chunk flushing overhead: every Suspense boundary incurs streaming protocol overhead (template tags, replacement scripts, and hydration markers); coarse boundaries reduce network chunk count and paint thrashing, while preserving sub-100ms TTFB for the primary reading content.
* **The Code Experiment**:
  * *Naive*: Wrapping the entire page in a single <Suspense> boundary (causing a blank screen until everything loads) or wrapping 50 tiny UI elements individually.
  * *React*: Structuring the page with an instant synchronous shell for headlines, and wrapping two cohesive asynchronous sections (<Comments /> and <RelatedStories />) in dedicated boundaries.
* **Scope Boundary**: Focus on boundary placement strategy, avoiding layout shifts, and balancing shell speed against fallback stability.
