# Brief 2026-09-20-21: The One-Thought Sentence Standard and Clause Chaining Ban

## 1. Context & Autopsy of the Defect

During review of the revised Lecture 4 (`md-lectures-revised/04.md`), the user flagged a long, clause-chained sentence in Step 2:

> *"Headline accepts title and renders a semantic h2 el-
> ement. Instead of the reserved JavaScript keyword class,
> it assigns className, adhering strictly to JSX naming con-
> ventions while mapping directly to the DOM property.
> 
> is there a rule agains tsuch large sentences? nnn"*

An autopsy revealed why this sentence failed:
1. **Four Chained Clauses in One Breath**: The sentence contained 23 words packed with:
   - A contrast clause (*"Instead of the reserved JavaScript keyword class,"*)
   - A main clause (*"it assigns className,"*)
   - A participial phrase (*"adhering strictly to JSX naming conventions"*)
   - A subordinate clause (*"while mapping directly to the DOM property."*)
2. **Mid-Word Print Hyphenation**: In the PDF layout, the breathless run-on caused awkward hyphenations ("el-ement", "con-ventions").
3. **Cognitive Overload for the B2 Tired Reader**: The reader is forced to hold three separate technical ideas (the keyword collision, the JSX convention, and the native DOM property mapping) in working memory before reaching a period.
4. **Average vs. Ceiling Loophole**: While our existing voice rule mandated an *average* sentence length of 12–18 words, it lacked a hard ceiling and an explicit ban on participial stacking, allowing bloated 23+ word sentences to slip through when offset by shorter neighbors.

## 2. Technical Decisions & Rules Added

We codified the One-Thought Sentence Standard across `skills/` under tag `#2026_09_20_21_group_1`:

1. **`skills/lecture-revision/SKILL.md` (Principle 03B & Gate 8)**:
   - Added Principle 03B (*The One-Thought Sentence Standard*):
     - Cap individual sentences at 20 words.
     - Ban clause chaining: express exactly one main idea per sentence.
     - Ban stacking contrast clauses, participial phrases, and subordinate clauses (*"Instead of X, it does Y, adhering to Z while mapping to W"*).
     - Mandate breaking multi-part explanations into punchy, active sentences of 7 to 15 words.
     - Included COUNTER-EXAMPLE (the 23-word sentence) and PROPER EXAMPLE (the 3-sentence split: 7, 7, and 15 words).
   - Updated Gate 8 (The Tired-Reader Gate) to verify that sentences are hard-capped at 20 words and free of chained participial clauses.

2. **`skills/lecture-voice/SKILL.md` (Principle 03)**:
   - Added the 20-word cap and clause-chaining ban to Principle 03: *"Cap individual sentences at 20 words and ban clause chaining: express exactly one main idea per sentence, and avoid chaining contrast clauses, participial phrases, and subordinate clauses into breathless run-ons."*

3. **`skills/verification/SKILL.md` (Gate 08)**:
   - Added sentence cap at 20 words and clause-chaining ban to Gate 08 (*The Developer-at-the-Keyboard gate*).

4. **`skills/RULE-TAGS.md`**:
