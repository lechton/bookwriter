# Brief 2026-10-11-04: The Introduction Teaches

## Why

After the rollback (brief 2026-10-11-03), the owner asked for a serious introduction built on a few general principles and approved examples, not on stacked rules. The old instructions pulled against that: seven opening beats that hid the mechanism behind a danger, a mystery, and a promise; a lead-in rule before the questions that grew into "In technical interviews, engineering candidates are closely evaluated..."; a five-step ritual for every new term; and a Lecture 1 runway example that named four ideas without showing any of them in code.

## Group 1: The introduction teaches (#2026_10_11_04_group_1)

- `lecture-structure` Section 12 opens with the introduction's role: the first section and the runway teach the ideas the questions stand on, by their proper names, from what the reader knows. The old four-point runway list (limitation, mechanism, surprise left as a question, mental model) and the Lecture 1 newsroom example are replaced by five standards: one idea at a time; name it and show it in the shortest code; say who does what; confirm with a second tiny example and a closing rule; only the words the idea needs.
- New file `lecture-structure/intro-examples.md`: owner-approved examples only, plus failed versions with reasons. It holds the imperative and declarative example (Lecture 1), the failed Lecture 1 runway, and the failed Lecture 7 lead-in.
- The `children` example the owner approved was first placed in the file, and Gemini pasted it word for word into Lecture 7 (built at 12:21 while this batch was half done). It was removed: an example leaves the file before its own lesson is built. The text is kept here for the record: "Content between component tags is called children", with `<Card><h2>Night Trains</h2></Card>`, `function Card({ children })`, a second card holding `<p>Hello!</p>`, and the closing sentence "Whatever you write between a component's tags reaches that component as its `children` prop." The imperative and declarative example must leave the file before Lecture 1 is built.
- `lecture-voice` Section 22 (five-step term progression) now points to the Section 12 standard: a large mechanism shows its problem first, a small term is named and shown. Section 23 keeps decoding symbols, both sides of passing, and browser versus React; etymology is gone; its proper example is a Lecture 6 prop.
- `pre-lecture` Section 02 loses its form-specific bullet. The blueprint's first section and runway are written fresh for each lesson, because Lecture 7's old blueprint carried its lead-in through two rebuilds.
- `verification` Section 21 gains reading gate 0: can a reader who knows only earlier lessons follow the introduction, with every new term shown in code next to its name?
- `produce_prompt.md` names the opening lines, the introduction standard, and the examples file, and asks for every code form the questions use (Lecture 7's JSX spread reached Question 3 untaught).
- `AGENTS.md`: the Organic Lexical Audit line asks for a concrete case the reader recognizes instead of "visceral, high-stakes scenarios".
