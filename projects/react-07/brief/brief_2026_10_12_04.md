# Brief 2026-10-12-04: Every Sentence Teaches the Subject

## Why

Lecture 1's opening contained the sentence "To answer it, we need one term first." The owner called it cringe: it describes what the lesson is about to do instead of doing it. The sentence projected the blueprint's plan (the paragraph carries the label `{term first}`) into the reader's text. The old standard 5 had a clause for this ("The text starts teaching in its first sentence"), and it was lost when standard 5 was reworded in brief 2026-10-12-02.

## Group 1: No meta narration (#2026_10_12_04_group_1)

- `lecture-structure` Section 12: the lost clause returns as standard 6, "Every sentence teaches the subject; none announces what the text is about to do", with the Lecture 1 sentence as the quoted case and the fix beside it ("The browser does not show your HTML file directly.").

## Not done

Nothing else. The same habit appeared twice more in Lecture 1 ("This is the first lesson, so the questions stand only on what you know from the web", and "Read it from the top."); those are fixed in the lecture itself under `ppp`, not here.
