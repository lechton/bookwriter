# Brief 2026-10-15-02: The Case Study Repeats the Theory

Status: problem report. No skill or lecture has been changed yet.

## Why

The owner read the demo Lecture 1 of react-08 and found the practical example weak: the step titles, the step headings, and the code comments work, but the section does not even start with an explanation, and something else feels off. The case study was never sharpened in react-07, so this report reads the react-08 demo against the skills and names the causes before any rule is written.

## What is wrong in the demo Lecture 1

1. **It starts with no explanation.** After `### Let's Design a Practical Example`, the reader gets a canvas, a files panel, and a figure, with no sentence saying what we build, why, or what to watch. The skill causes it: `lecture-structure` Section 15 says "Stage A opens with the Rendered UI Canvas", and the author followed it to the letter.

2. **It teaches nothing new.** The finished `SiteHeader` (greeting, corner, search box) was shown whole in the runway, before Question 1. Questions 2 and 3 showed it again. The three steps then cut the same ten lines into "add the greeting", "add the corner", "add the search box" and rebuild them. That is the artificial shredding `lecture-structure` Section 16 warns against: no step makes a decision, and the reader assembles something they have already seen finished.

3. **It is too small to show why it matters.** The bank's hook says the name appears "in ten places". The case study is where that scale should finally arrive, and it stays at two places. With two places, "one forgotten line" feels unlikely. With five places and a second value, the imperative script visibly grows while the React component does not.

4. **The steps do not follow the thread.** The steps happen to run greeting, corner, search box, but nothing tells the reader that Step 1 is Principle 1, Step 2 is Principle 2, and Step 3 is Principle 3. The case study could replay the thread aloud, and the whole lesson would then have one shape: the spoken preview announces the thread, the questions teach it, the case study applies it, and the summary repeats it.

5. **No step shows its result.** Each step ends on code. None ends with what the screen or the Elements panel now shows, which is exactly the proof the lesson trained the reader to look for.

6. **The ending repeats itself.** The architecture audit figure, the closing comparison table, and the summary each restate "commands against one description". The component summary also explains `main.jsx`, which no step built.

## Proposed rebuild of Lecture 1's case study

- **One framing paragraph before the canvas**, two or three sentences: we now build the full header from the opening, with the name in several places and an unread-message count; the three steps follow the lesson's three principles in order; keep the dev server running and the Elements panel open.
- **A bigger example inside the scope** (one component, no props, no state): `userName` in three places (the greeting, the corner, the account menu, "Mary Brooks's account"), `unreadCount` in two places ("Messages (3)" and "You have 3 new messages"), and the search box. A number in curly braces is already taught by the runway's `SiteFooter`.
- **Steps that each make one decision, labelled with the thread:**

| Step | Principle | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The core mechanism | the whole header described from two variables, with no DOM calls; ends with the Elements panel showing the header inside the empty root |
| 2 | Describing many places from one value | five places read two variables; one sentence counts what plain JavaScript would need: five update lines for every change |
| 3 | Describing the page again when the value changes | type in the search box, change both values, save; React changes exactly the changed texts (count to be verified in Chrome) and keeps the box |

- **The pipeline figure's cards carry the same three labels**, so Figure 1.1 shows the thread instead of repeating the step headings.
- **A shorter ending:** the audit figure walks through the three steps' results; the comparison table keeps its "When it fits" row; `main.jsx` leaves the role panel or stays as a one-line boundary.

## Instruction causes to fix when the thread instructions are written

- `lecture-structure` Section 15: prune "Stage A opens with the Rendered UI Canvas" so a short framing paragraph comes first, and say what that paragraph holds (what we build, why, what each step proves, what to keep open).
- `lecture-structure` Section 16 and `pre-lecture` Section 15: each step applies one principle, in the thread's order, and makes one decision the reader has not watched yet; a step that only re-adds lines already shown in the runway or a question is shredding.

- `lecture-structure` Section 16 and `map-principles` Section 06: the case study delivers the scale the bank's hook promises (the "ten places"), within the scope, so the example grows beyond the questions' minimal code.
