# Brief 2026-10-15-06: The Plain Website Setting

## Why

Lecture 5 opened with "The Town News front page has grown: a masthead, a story with its byline, and a box of the most-read stories". Masthead and byline were banned in three places (`lecture-voice` Sections 03 and 26, and `produce_prompt.md`), and gate 11 of `verification` asked for "no newsroom jargon", yet the words reached the lecture.

The bans acted on the lecture, while the names were settled upstream. 158 of the 180 syllabus cards are set in a newsroom. `map-principles` Section 01 made the card the authority for the code experiment, and Section 06 asked the proofs to use the card's components and variables, so map 05 kept `NewsHeader`, `EditorialFeed`, `EditorialByline`, and "the Town News front page". The produce prompt then called the map the contract to copy word for word and, in the same paragraph, asked the lecture to rename its story names. The author copied. The law's own examples also taught a news site (`alt="Town News"` in `code-blocks` 06B, `NewsCard` in `intro-examples`, "On a news site" in `lecture-voice` 24), and handouts and earlier lectures carried Town News forward.

A themed setting brings its trade's words with it: a front page has a masthead, bylines, and an edition. A ban list cannot keep up with a setting that keeps supplying words, so the fix replaces the setting and moves the naming to the one place where names are settled.

## Group 1: The Plain Website Setting (#2026_10_15_06_group_1)

- `lecture-voice` Section 03: the newsroom-lore bullet and the banned-name bullet become one positive standard. Each part of the page is named by what the reader sees on any website. The page stays a plain list of articles with no made-up site name, and a lesson whose problem lives in another everyday widget (a ticket order, a task list, a profile form, a feedback form) uses that widget, as Section 25 already describes. The names follow the corrected archive of lectures 01 to 21 (`SiteHeader`, `ArticleList`, `ArticleCard`, `StoryCard`, `LikeButton`, `TicketOrder`). A proper and a counter-example pair use Lecture 5's opening.
- `lecture-voice` Section 24: the proper example says "a website with articles" instead of "a news site".
- `lecture-voice` Section 26: the Banned Publishing Jargon line and the masthead example of the 8-Year-Old Test are pruned; Section 03 owns the standard.
- `map-principles` Section 01: the card decides what the code experiment builds and breaks; the map names it by `lecture-voice` Section 03 in every field the lecture copies or follows.
- `map-principles` Section 06: the proofs build on the map's one example named that way; the proper example keeps `StoryCard`, and map 05 is added as a counter-example.
- `produce_prompt.md`: the lecture keeps the map's names and reports a map field that still carries a trade word instead of renaming it; the third ban list becomes one pointer to `lecture-voice` Section 03.
- `verification` gate 11 points to `lecture-voice` Section 03 instead of naming newsroom jargon.
- `code-blocks` Section 06B and `intro-examples` (the Lecture 7 cold drop): the news-site names in the examples become plain ones.
