# Brief 2026-10-12-01: The Theme and Its Options

## Why

The introduction standard of 2026-10-11 fixed how an idea is said (warm, plain, never cold), but not why the reader meets it. Lecture 7 still presented `children` as the answer to a question the reader had not asked, and showed the props from Lecture 6 only so they could fail ("let's see why the props from Lecture 6 are not enough"). The owner's remedy: every introduction opens with a general theme (a question every developer meets), presents the different options, and says which one the chapter covers. For Lecture 7 the theme is how one card shows different content: `<ContentCard name="Paul" />` with a prop, or `<ContentCard><p>My name is Paul</p></ContentCard>` with `children`. The questions then show the chosen option's forms, and the end of the lesson says when each option fits.

Three choices, made where the owner had not yet answered:

- Options are required only where they are real. A fixed list to fill would make the writer invent options, the same cause as Question 5 and the badge (brief 2026-10-11-06). When React offers no rival way, the options are the habit without React and what React does (Lecture 1's imperative and declarative).
- The map carries the theme and its options, so the lecture follows them instead of inventing them while writing.
- The owner's Lecture 7 sentence stays, now as the third step after the theme and the two options. The closing uses the existing comparison table instead of a new section.
