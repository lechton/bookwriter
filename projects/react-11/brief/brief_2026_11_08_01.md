# Brief 2026-11-08 01: Surveillance against invented jargon

## Why

Audit [21_2026_11_08_001](../audit/21_2026_11_08_001.md) found that Lecture 21 used "picture" 94 times. The word is in neither react.dev nor MDN. The map invented it as a gloss for the docs' term "visual state" ("one complete picture the user can see"), and the lecture copied it for `status` and its values. The reader had to translate it back into code every time.

The earlier projects had a vocabulary gate that worked, and it never reached v10.5:
- react-03 `AUDIT-CHECKLIST.md` Gate 2, the Standard React Vocabulary Gate: only standard React and web platform vocabulary; never turn a situation into a fake proper noun or buzzword, such as "controlled form illusion" or "keystroke tracking fatigue". It was carried in the old-instructions of react-04 to react-09.
- react-09 v9.4 `pre-lecture/SKILL.md` Section 10: standard industry terminology, never private or invented terms, with "UI snappers" in place of "components" as the bad example; and the vocabulary locked in the blueprint.
- react-09 v9.1 `lecture-voice` Sections 22 to 26: the exact term with its plain meaning, the same term everywhere, no pseudo-academic buzzwords, and no designer or specialist words.

v10.5 had only "Do not invent labels" in editing-skills, which is for skill writers, and a `## Terms` list in the map that nothing checked.

## What changed

1. `skills/v10.5/lecture/SKILL.md`: a new section, *Only the words of React and the web*. Allowed: the words of https://react.dev and MDN, standard computer science terms such as "state machine" when the lesson really needs them, and everyday words in their everyday sense. A name made up by the writer is jargon, even when it sounds plain. An everyday word that starts to stand for something in the code has become jargon. Examples from react-03, react-09, and Lecture 21.
2. `skills/v10.5/map/SKILL.md`: `## Terms` says where each term is found (the react.dev or MDN page, "computer science term", or "everyday word"); a term found in none of these does not go in the map; the lecture may use as names only the terms on the list. New question 7 in *Before you finish*.
3. `skills/v10.5/check/SKILL.md`: Step B, in the "Too much" read, replaces every word used as a name that is not in `## Terms` and is not an everyday word in its everyday sense, and reports it. Step A does the same.

## Dropped on the way

A rewrite of lecture rule 6 to "call each thing by its code name", a third read of the draft, and a separate word check limited to titles, headings, bold words, and captions.
