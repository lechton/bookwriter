# Brief 2026-11-07 03: Write from what the reader knows, and let each sentence grow from the last

## Why this batch exists

The owner found Lecture 20 harder to read under the newer rules and asked that the skills follow the reader's theory of mind: what the reader knows at each point, with every sentence flowing from the one before. The owner gave a rewrite of the Lecture 20 opening as the model. It starts from `name` and `setName`, which the reader knows from Lesson 12, says again what each one does on this page, and then takes one step per sentence: `name` starts empty, so the page shows nothing, so the box bound to `name` is blank too. `onChange` comes in through `onClick`, which the reader already knows, and the conclusion comes last.

The Lecture 20 opening written under `v10.4` does the opposite. "Its handler calls `setName` with an empty string" assumes the reader still has the code in mind. Then come four short sentences that are each true but do not lead into each other, a question the reader cannot yet answer, and two definitions of `onChange` and `value`.

The cause was in the skills:
- `writing` Section 1 described the reader only as tired, "B2 English for a reader who has a headache". It said nothing about what the reader knows, so it pushed toward shorter sentences, not toward connected ones.
- `writing` Section 7 said "One idea per sentence" and asked to split any sentence with "because", "which", or "while". That cut the links between ideas. Brief 2026-11-07 02 already noted that the owner's approved passage breaks this rule.
- `writing` Section 3 asked for the facts before a question "one fact per sentence", which produced rows of separate sentences.
- `writing` Section 2 said to use words from earlier lessons freely, so the writer started from `setName` without saying again what it does on this page.
- `check` Step B cut "long sentences" but never asked whether sentences connect. The build's 28-word warning pushes the same way.

Following Prune Before Banning, the batch rewrites Section 1 and narrows or replaces the rules above. No new section and no new ban were added.

## The groups

### #2026_11_07_03_group_1: Write from what the reader knows, and let each sentence grow from the last
- `writing` Section 1: the reader knows what earlier lessons taught and what this lecture has said so far, and nothing else. Start each explanation from something the reader holds, add one new thing, let the next sentence pick it up, bring a new idea in through a known one, and save the conclusion for the end. A name from nowhere, or a row of short sentences that do not lead into each other, has failed.
- PROPER: the owner's rewrite of the Lecture 20 opening, 2026-11-07. Its two em dashes were turned into commas, because the workspace bans em dashes.
- COUNTER-EXAMPLE: the Lecture 20 opening written under `v10.4`.
- `writing` Section 11, question 4: "Can every sentence be understood on the first read?" becomes "Does every sentence follow from the one before, and does each new thing rest on something the reader already holds at that point?". The section heading now says "the reader of Section 1".

### #2026_11_07_03_group_2: Prune the rules that chop sentences apart
- `writing` Section 7: "One idea per sentence. When a sentence adds a reason or a second event with "because", "which", or "while", make it two sentences." becomes "Let each sentence follow from the one before. A sentence may carry its own reason with "because", "so", or "when", or a colon, when that is how the thought goes. Split a sentence when it glues together things that do not belong together, such as a reason, a choice, and a comparison at once. Short sentences that do not connect are as hard to read as long ones." The Lecture 04 PROPER and COUNTER-EXAMPLE under it still fit and are unchanged.
- `writing` Section 3: "The tired reader has forgotten the picture above, so give them everything again, one fact per sentence, in the order it happens" becomes "The reader may have forgotten the picture above, so give them everything again, in the order it happens, with each fact leading into the next".
- `writing` Section 2: under words from earlier lessons, a new sentence says that when an explanation starts from such a word, the writer says what it does on this page first, as the first step of the story and not as a definition.
- `writing` Sections 4 and 7: "the tired reader" and "a tired reader" become "the reader" and "often", so one term names the reader.

### #2026_11_07_03_group_3: The reads and the skill rules use the same reader
- `check` Step A: the writer reads as the reader of `writing` Section 1, who knows only the earlier lessons and what the lecture has said so far.
- `check` Step B: under "too much", "Long sentences, two ideas glued together" becomes "Sentences that glue together things that do not belong together". Under "something missing", a sentence that does not follow from the one before, or a new thing that rests on nothing the reader holds yet, is added.
- `skill-surveillance` item 1: "Use B2 English and short sentences" becomes "Use plain English, and let each sentence follow from the one before".
