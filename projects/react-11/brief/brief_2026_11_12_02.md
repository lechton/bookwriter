# Brief 2026-11-12 02: A lecture is built from a prelecture of definitions and principles

## Why

The owner's words, 2026-11-12:

> "Now I understand we have elements we can name quanta of information. These are fundamentally of two types: definitions (every key concept, effectively, can be presented as a definition, and the definitions together are complete, in the sense that they connect all the dots), and principles, which are operational principles: do this and do not do that. So in theory we could reconstruct the process and focus on definitions and principles. If we start with concrete definitions and verify there are no errors, then models can build with definitions and principles better."

> "I want each lecture to bother writing definitions custom-made, to some extent, to the philosophy of the lecture at hand. Definitions need to be pertinent to the lecture."

> "I want an emphasis on variable names that are easy to read, e.g. `count`, `setCount`, `score`, `setScore`. Terms like `order` and `setOrder` are confusing: is order a verb? Is it a noun? Make it clearer, e.g. `item`, `setItem`. We need MAXIMUM clarity on variables, setters, and functions, as they will be part of the definitions, read with virtually no context."

> "The leading-up definitions, as prerequisites, also need to be custom-made, inspired by previous iterations, but if possible written in a fashion that adapts, to the extent that is meaningful, to the level of each chapter."

> "We have in this project two files: the prelecture and the lecture. In the prelecture, the model writes the definitions by respecting the instructions and examples as found in the notes `definitions.md` and `definitions-guide.md`. It also adds the elements that you judge need to be added, based on your previous analysis and previous experience."

> "Definitions can be at the top as reminders, but some of the reminders can be placed ad hoc in the text, when it is pertinent to remind something. It is not an absolute rule of being at the top; it is a general direction."

> "Edit v11.1 directly. Yes, proceed as you consider wise, per your discernment."

The evidence came from Lecture 16. Of its nine versions, `16-gemini3.8-v1` read best. `16-gemini3.8-v2` held the owner's own definitions, but its new definitions sat before the screen, and the step-by-step walk and the fixed code left the opening. `16-gemini3.8-v1` kept the screen, but said each new idea twice, in the text and in a box.

## Sentences that are mine, not the owner's

1. "A definition says what a thing is and how React behaves. A principle says what to do and what not to do."
2. The word ranges in the guide are "the room a definition needs to unpack every step, not a target to hit".
3. Each definition "leads toward the key idea of this lecture, and uses this lecture's screen and names".
4. Reminders are marked `[REVIEW]` and new ideas `[NEW]`, the labels the build already draws.
5. "Each new definition appears once, at the moment the screen needs it, either in a definition box or in the text, never in both." This came from the Lecture 16 analysis, and the owner did not object.
6. The eight other parts of the prelecture (names, terms, screen, opening, principles with their proof on the screen, verdicts, order, lab), from the earlier analysis, as the owner asked.
7. The four exceptions in `lecture` and `check`: reminders instead of "do not teach an earlier lesson again", the question answered in the very next words, the owner's signposts inside a definition, and "(e.g. the score)" inside a box.
8. The owner's approval of each prelecture before the lecture, unless the owner says "ppp".
9. The second read now fixes the lecture in place, instead of writing a new file from a draft.
