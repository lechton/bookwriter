# 005 — Design System (Single Source of Truth)

> **Status: ACTIVE.** Supersedes 001–004. Files `001.md` (Dark Mode Modern), `002.md` (condensed dark-mode prompt), `003.md` (Neo-Brutalist paper, paragraph form), and `004.md` (Neo-Brutalist Tailwind HTML system prompt) are retained in this folder as historical options for reference only — do not use them for new work. This file is the complete and only authority on how diagrams should look and behave.

## 0. The Reference Exemplar

The gold standard for what these diagrams should look like is a real, shipped deck at `diagram-lab/docs/Gemini experiments/01-cards-02/svelte_5_25_flashcards.html`. Open it and study it. Every diagram in that file averages two to three visual elements, uses flat Tailwind Neo-Brutalism, and resolves its concept in a single glance. New diagrams must match that simplicity. If yours needs more than four distinct elements, the concept is too big for one card — split it.

## 1. Aesthetic

Flat Neo-Brutalism on paper. Not dark mode, not glassmorphism, not 3D, not soft-glow. The look is high-contrast, industrial, and intentionally blunt. An off-white paper background with an optional faint dotted grid is the canvas; everything else sits on top as a rigid rectangle with a heavy black outline and a hard offset shadow.

## 2. Color Palette

* Background: off-white paper (`#faf7f0` or `bg-yellow-50`), with an optional faint dotted grid.
* Outlines, text, and connection lines: solid black, thick (`border-4 border-black`, or 3–4px strokes).
* Primary accent — reserved for the ONE focal point per diagram (key node, terminal signal, the single most important element): flat muted mustard-yellow (`bg-yellow-300` / `#facc15` / `#d4a017`).
* Secondary accent — for intermediate points, status pills, transformation indicators: flat desaturated cyan or teal (`bg-cyan-100` / `#22d3ee` / `#0e7490`). Must never compete with the primary.
* Neutral fills: pure white for standard data containers.
* Validation colors (red/green used as pass/fail): forbidden. They grade instead of teach. Use keywords, icons, and asymmetry to carry the same meaning.

## 3. Typography

* All code, variables, and technical tokens: monospace (`font-mono` / JetBrains Mono), bold, solid black.
* Headings and labels: clean geometric sans-serif (Space Grotesk / Inter), bold.
* Micro-labels and badges: `text-[10px] font-bold uppercase tracking-widest`, usually on a black or accent fill with a black border, often absolutely positioned (`absolute -top-3`) to label a container.

## 4. Components

* Containers: rigid rectangles, `border-4 border-black`, with a hard solid black drop shadow offset down and to the right (`shadow-[4px_4px_0_0_#000]` or `6px` for emphasis). No blurred shadows. No outer glow.
* Badges and pills: small rectangles or pills with a distinct accent fill and a black border, used to label or annotate a container.
* Browser chrome: the outermost container optionally carries three traffic-light dots in the top-left corner to signal that the surface is a UI environment.
* Arrows: thick black lines (2–4px), terminating in a solid triangular arrowhead. Straight lines or single-bend orthogonal routes only — no SVG curves, no gradient-filled glowing pipes, no diagonal sweeps.

## 5. Layout Patterns (pick exactly one per diagram)

* Side-by-side comparison (A vs B) — two boxes, one accent each.
* Linear pipeline or flowchart — two to four boxes left-to-right or top-to-bottom, arrows between.
* Single hero card — one emphasized box with one or two supporting labels.
* Bar or scale comparison — two bars of different heights or lengths.
* Timeline — two to four phases on a horizontal line.

## 6. The Complexity Budget (hard rule)

Maximum four distinct visual elements per diagram. An element is a box, card, arrow, or labeled group. Captions and micro-labels attached to an existing element do not count separately. If the concept cannot be expressed in four elements, it is too large for one card: either split it across two cards, or move the nuance into the answer text where it belongs. The diagram's job is one clean visual metaphor, not a lecture. This budget is the single biggest difference between a clean diagram and a convoluted one.

## 7. Pedagogical Rules

* Mechanism over decoration. Every box and connection must teach a specific mechanical truth — a data flow, a scope boundary, a component hierarchy. No generic abstract art.
* Prescriptive, not merely descriptive. The diagram should argue which operations are heard, correct, or preferred — not just depict what happens to exist.
* No red/green validation framing. The diagram teaches a mechanism; it does not grade an outcome.
* Asymmetry, size, and keywords carry meaning before color does. Reach for layout position and labels first; reach for color last.

## 8. The Generic Prompt (prepend to every image generation)

> Neo-Brutalist Technical Diagram Style Prompt. A high-contrast, flat design technical diagram set against a clean, off-white background with a slight paper texture. All elements (cards, containers, lines, arrows) feature a thick, heavy black outline to create a stark, bold interface. Primary Accent: a flat, muted mustard-yellow used strictly for the single most critical focal point. Secondary Accent: a flat, desaturated cyan or teal used for intermediate connection points, status pills, or transformation indicators. Neutral Elements: pure white fills for standard data containers. Typography and Lines: crisp, solid black monospaced font for all text and code; bold, thick black lines for connections, distinct from the outlines of the containers. Containers: rigid rectangles with bold outlines and a heavy, stark black drop shadow offset down and to the right for depth. Indicators: pill-shaped or circular badges with distinct fill colors and bold outlines. Structure: minimalist and industrial; the main content is enclosed in a stylized browser window outline with three simple traffic-light dots in the top-left corner; titles are bold, large, and underscored with a heavy black line. Hard limit: at most four distinct visual elements in the entire graphic — refuse to add a fifth.
