## Why This Batch Exists

Chapter 1 of `tort-01` (`md-lectures/01-chapter-01.md`) was authored as a rich written text with historical backstories, in-line parenthetical definitions, and `> **Streetwise Exam Advice:**` boxes. To transform this chapter (and future Tort Law chapters) into an organic, natural **audio lecture**, the owner requested a dedicated experimental skillset drawing on:
1. **VideoMax's spoken-audio and oral-structure standards** (`/Users/techton/lechton/research-code/videomax/skills/v1.2/oral-structure/SKILL.md` and `lecture-scripting/SKILL.md`), so that the text unfolds like a real tutor thinking aloud with a student rather than a written textbook read out loud.
2. **Text-dependent legal audio rules** tailored to how a listener absorbs and remembers common law cases by ear:
   - **Single-Name Case Anchoring and Repetition (`Alex v Jones` ➔ `Alex`)**: Introduce the full two-party case citation and year once, then repeat only one short key name throughout the story, the principle, and all later callbacks so the listener links the facts and rule to that single name in memory.
   - **Incremental Block Revision (`> **Revision:**` replacing `> **Streetwise Exam Advice:**`)**: At the end of each block of two or three cases (never after every single case), review the previous cases incrementally in short, easy-to-grasp spoken sentences using their single short name and year, and close with the open question that leads into the next case.
