# Brief 2026-11-05 01: Owner Priorities for the Audio Lecture and a Barnett Sample (`#2026_11_05_01_group_1`)

## What the Owner Said

After listening to the Chapter 1 audio text, the owner found each section boring: rich in information, but told in a straight line. The owner settled three points so they are not raised again. First, the total length is not the problem. Second, repeating the short case name is wanted, and more repetition could help, because a listener forgets a name two sentences after hearing it. Third, the key cause is that the background comes before the reason to care. The owner also found it interesting that the best moments of a case (twists, shocking lines, close votes) are told in passing.

## Follow-up the Same Day

The owner approved the structure of the Barnett sample but found the many very short sentences overwhelming. Section 1.1 of the skill now records the approved structure and a flowing-sentence priority, with the owner's own example, and the feedback log has row 6. A second draft, [samples/sample-01-barnett-v2.md](../skills/audio-lecture/samples/sample-01-barnett-v2.md), keeps the structure, joins linked thoughts into flowing sentences, and corrects the wording of the but for test. The choppy style comes partly from Rule 2.1 ("break long written sentences") and the spoken demo, which both model very short sentences; they are left as they are until Section 2 is revised.

The owner then found the v2 sentences far too long, quoting its opening sentence. The point in Section 1.1 now reads "One breath per sentence": each sentence carries one idea, or two ideas joined by a simple link word, and the skill shows both extremes beside the fixed version. A third draft, [samples/sample-01-barnett-v3.md](../skills/audio-lecture/samples/sample-01-barnett-v3.md), averages about 15 words per sentence, with the longest at 25. Feedback log row 7.

The owner then asked for three more standards, now in Section 1.1 with examples. First, plain B2 English for a listener who has never studied tort law, with every unusual word in the facts and every key term, legal term, court, and judicial role explained. Second, every answer restates its question, because a listener does not remember it. Third, turns in the story are announced plainly, not with clever images. A fourth draft, [samples/sample-01-barnett-v4.md](../skills/audio-lecture/samples/sample-01-barnett-v4.md), applies all three. Feedback log row 8.
