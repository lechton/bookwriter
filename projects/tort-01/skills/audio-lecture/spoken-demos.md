# Spoken Demo: Written versus Spoken

Read this once before writing, to hear the difference. Take the rhythm, not the wording.

WRITTEN (before):
```text
In Vaughan v Menlove (1837), the defendant built a hayrick (a stack of damp hay, which can ignite through fermentation) near the claimant's cottages and, despite warnings, failed to remove it; he argued he should be judged by his own limited intelligence.
```

SPOKEN (after):
```text
Come back with me to Shropshire in the 1830s, to Vaughan v Menlove, decided in 1837. We'll call it Vaughan. Every farmer then knew one danger. If you stack hay while it is still wet, it heats up inside, and one day it can catch fire on its own. In Vaughan, a farmer built a damp haystack right next to his neighbour's cottages. People warned him for five weeks. He said he would chance it. The stack caught fire, and the cottages burned down. In court, his lawyer said something remarkable. My client is not very clever, he said, so judge him by his own best effort. Now stop and think. If that were the rule, who would ever pay? The court in Vaughan said no. You are measured against an ordinary sensible person, not against yourself.
```

What changed: the scene comes first, each sentence holds one thought, the technical idea is explained in plain words inside the story, the short name Vaughan is repeated, and the tutor asks the student a real question before giving the rule.
