# Brief 2026-11-08 03: The Opening Essence starts from the question, and a way of working with no short surprise opens in plain words

## Why

The opening of Lecture 21 was rewritten three times. The 2026-11-07 version started with `error.style.display = ''` and a sentence on CSS display. The 2026-11-08 morning version started with `status`, visual states, three pieces, and two kinds of input. Both put details before the question they answer. The owner then wrote the approved opening: how do we change what a page shows, the theme button, the two ways, the React way as two steps (the element depends on a variable, and we change only the variable), and what is at stake.

Move 1 of the Opening Essence caused the first failure: it asked for the naked mechanism in its smallest code, every time. That fits syntax lessons, and code-first lessons whose short code gives a surprise, such as Lectures 12, 15, and 16. It does not fit a lesson whose idea is the shape of a way of working. Lecture 01, Example 3 of the skill, already opened that way, but the skill never named it as a form.

## What changed

Group 1, `skills/v10.6/opening-essence/SKILL.md`:
1. The description says "the naked idea, in its simplest lines or in plain words".
2. Sin 1 also names low-level details: a DOM command, a handler, and a setter before the reader knows the question.
3. Move 1 starts from the lesson's question and has two forms of the naked idea: the smallest code when a few lines show the idea, including a short code with a surprise; plain words when the idea is the shape of a way of working and no short code shows it with a surprise. The old text of move 1 is kept under the first form.
4. Section 2a says what is at stake: what the user sees on the page when the idea is ignored, said right after the naked idea. Row 3 of its table points to Example 8.
5. New Example 8: the approved Lecture 21 opening as the PROPER example, the two rejected openings as counter-examples, Example 3 named as its sibling, and one line that keeps the theme button as Lecture 21's own case. Section 3 says "eight" examples.
6. Checklist: the first question asks for the question and the naked idea in either form; the last question adds what is at stake for the user.

Group 2:
1. `skills/v10.6/moves/smallest-code-first.md`: the move is step 1 of the Opening Essence when a few lines of code show the idea.
2. `skills/v10.6/map/SKILL.md`: the map's Start of the Lecture records what is at stake, and the idea in plain words when no short code shows it.

Group 3: this brief, its row in `01_TOC.md`, and three rows in `skills/v10.6/RULE-TAGS.md`.
