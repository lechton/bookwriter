# Brief 2026-11-07 01: Lay out the facts before the question, and name each version by its difference

## Why this batch exists

The owner compared Lecture 20, written under `v10.4`, with Lecture 19, and found Lecture 20 harder to follow even where it explains the right thing. The test case was the opening of Principle 2, "Why the box with `defaultValue` keeps John":

> While the user types, the two boxes behave exactly the same. Only Clear shows the difference. So why does the left box keep John?

A first correction added more sentences, and the owner, reading as a tired and confused reader, still found it confusing. The second correction was approved.

The cause was in `writing` Section 3. It said "Ask the reader's question first", but it never said what must come before the question. So the writer asked the question while the facts behind it were still in a picture above, in summary words such as "behave the same" and "tells them apart", or in an earlier section the reader has forgotten. A tired reader cannot see why the result is strange, so the question does not land.

A sweep of the other skills found the same habit taught in more places:
- The `figures` preview example labels its boxes Box A, Box B, and Box C, and the `writing` examples then explain them as "box B" and "box C". Lecture 20 followed this with "the left column", "the right column", and "both versions" six times.
- The `map` field Surprises and their steps used the question "Why does box B refuse the letter?" as its example, and asked for no facts before the question.
- `check` Step B listed what a final read must add, but not a question without its facts or a version named by its place.

The sweep also found text left behind by earlier batches: `check` and the production prompt still spoke of "the five questions" in `writing` Section 11, which has six, and the `v10.4` README still described the skills with the `v10.3` words New code words and "the reader's question comes first".

Following Fix before you add, every change goes into an existing section. No new section was made.
