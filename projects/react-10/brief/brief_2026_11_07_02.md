# Brief 2026-11-07 02: Character from a fact, and references that point to one thing

## Why this batch exists

The owner asked for lectures written like a professional who also writes a good blog: technically precise and not boring. Three rewrites of one Lecture 20 passage, the Clear button in Principle 2, showed where character comes from and where it does not:
- A rewrite that named every thing in full in every sentence was precise and "unbearably boring".
- A rewrite built on short dramatic fragments, punchlines, and "React walks away" was called AI slop.
- A rewrite built on one fact the reader does not expect was approved. The fact: in HTML, the `value` attribute is the starting text, which the browser calls `input.defaultValue`, while React uses `value` for the text in the box right now and renames the starting text `defaultValue`.

So character comes from content, not from phrasing. A writer cannot invent such a fact while writing, so the map must find it in the sources and hand it over.

The owner also flagged "After Clear" as a typical failure: it names nothing. After the user clicks the Clear button, or after the developer adds it? The skills asked for each version to be named by its difference, but said nothing about actions and principles.

Following Fix before you add, every change goes into an existing section. `writing` Section 5 is widened rather than replaced, and its everyday-picture rule and examples stay as they were.
