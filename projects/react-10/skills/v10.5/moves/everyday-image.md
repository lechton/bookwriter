# Everyday Image #2026_11_07_11_group_2

## When to use it

Use it when the lesson brings a new word that sounds academic, such as *imperative*, *declarative*, *spread*, or *controlled*.

## How to do it

1. Give the word one image from everyday life.
2. Go straight back to the code, and keep using the code's names.
3. Use the image once. Do not repeat the image word in place of the code's names.

## A good and a bad example

**PROPER** (Lecture 01):

> Why do developers call plain JavaScript "imperative" and React "declarative"? Both words sound academic at first, but their meaning comes straight from everyday life. *Imperative* comes from the same root as an emperor giving commands: "Go find the paragraph! Now change its text!" *Declarative* means you simply declare what the finished page should look like, the way you hand a finished plan to a builder.

**PROPER** (Lecture 09):

> Think of the `article` object as a closed box holding the title, the subtitle, and the content. With the three dots `...`, JavaScript opens the box and spreads all of its items out across the tag for you.

**COUNTER-EXAMPLE** (a rejected draft):

> `{...article}` spreads every property of `article` onto `props`.

*Why this fails*: It uses the word *spreads* to explain the spread.
