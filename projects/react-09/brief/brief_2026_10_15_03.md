# Brief 2026-10-15-03: The Principle Thread

## Why

The owner listens to the lessons as well as reading them. A list of principles read aloud sounds like separate slogans: a listener cannot scan back up the page, so each new title must be decoded on its own, and by the third one the first is gone. The owner worked out a better shape with Gemini on Lecture 10 (`various/principles-demo/`): every principle of a lesson varies one anchor idea, the principles follow one axis, each label starts with the same words, and the lesson says the whole thread aloud once before the first question and once at the end. React-08 was opened as the experiment for it (react-07 stays frozen), and Lecture 1 was rebuilt by hand as the demo. The owner asked for perfection rather than economy, and for the map, not habit, to decide the order of the principles.

The demo then taught three more things. A role label such as "The core mechanism" says what a principle does in the list, not what it says, so a listener learns nothing from it; the owner had it renamed to "Describing what the page shows, not how to change it". The labels gain an interview term where one exists ("Declarative programming: describing what the page shows, not how to change it"). And the case study is where the thread is applied, not taught again: it combines the principles in one new, larger situation, one step per principle (brief 2026-10-15-02).

A study of the 21 react-07 maps and the 180-question bank found that the shape holds for every lesson with adaptations: the anchor can be a mechanism, a choice, a process, or a catalog; a lesson with seven or eight principles groups them under the axis; and a principle that does not vary the anchor (stopPropagation in Lecture 13) comes after the thread instead of breaking it. Those findings are written into the skills below as standards with examples, not as bans.

Every rule below is a standard to meet, shown on Lecture 1 and on the cases the study found. Where an older rule contradicted the thread (the principles heading goes "straight into Question 1"; the case study opens "with the Rendered UI Canvas"; Stage E always ends on an audit table), the older rule was pruned rather than fenced with a new ban.

## Group 1: The thread in the map (#2026_10_15_03_group_1)

- `map-principles` Section 04B (new): the map adds `## Principle Thread` after Theme and Options, with Anchor, Axis, Why This Axis, Spoken Preview, and Closing Line, and each principle adds `Thread Step` and `Rule`. The anchor names the one idea every principle varies; the axis is the order a listener can predict (the life of one value, cardinality, one operation per principle, time, code parts in order); the order of the principles is the order of the axis, which must also be the order of dependency, and the map records the axes it rejected. Anchor kinds: mechanism, choice, process, catalog. A side principle that does not vary the anchor comes after the thread, named as such. A lesson with many principles groups them by the axis. Proper example: map 01; half way: Gemini's Lecture 10 thread with "The core mechanism"; counter-example: the archived Lecture 1 list of separate slogans.
- `map-principles` Section 04: the order bullet now points to the axis.
- `map-principles` Section 12: the template carries the new section and fields; the Completion Gate gains a thread line.
- `map-principles` Section 13: the Sense Test reads the Spoken Preview aloud once.
- `src/diagnose-map.mjs`: `Principle Thread` is a required section; its five fields must be filled; every principle needs `Thread Step` and `Rule`; each part of every principle title must appear in the Spoken Preview, in principle order.

## Group 2: Labels a listener can follow (#2026_10_15_03_group_2)

- `map-principles` Section 04B: each principle title is a thread label: it repeats the anchor's opening words (anaphora) and says what this principle changes about it. When interviewers and the docs have an exact term for the principle, the label starts with it, then a colon and the plain meaning ("Single source of truth: describing many places from one value"). No term is forced: a label goes without one when no common term fits exactly, when the term is the lesson's subject already, or when a later lesson owns it. A label names what the principle says, never its role in the list ("The core mechanism", "The edge case").
- `principle-definition` Section 01: the box title is the map's label, and the italic line under it is the map's `Rule`; the old title standard (one claim, words the reader has, a rule the reader can follow) now applies to that rule line. The casebook titles stay valid as rule lines. Syntax section: the rule line's form.

## Group 4: The case study combines the principles (#2026_10_15_03_group_4)
