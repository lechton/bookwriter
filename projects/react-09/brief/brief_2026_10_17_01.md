# Brief 2026-10-17-01: The Summary Edition Skills (Revision 3)

## Why

Over one long session the owner and the agent turned Lecture 10 from the full lecture (about 6,600 words, 53 pages) into a short visual lesson, through two editions. Revision 2 (`03-01-md-revised-2`) cut the lecture to about 1,050 words with strong figures, but a cold reader found 34 places where it stopped standing on its own. Revision 3 (`04-01-md-revised-3`) kept the visual summary and added what a reader who never saw the full lecture needs: a worked example first, the components and their data, the shape of each idea drawn as a tree or hierarchy, stakes for every comparison, facts before their use, one consistent running example, and the conditions every description carries. Along the way the owner tried and rejected five design themes, and settled the look of the component panels. This batch writes that experience down as three skills, so the next lecture goes from the finished lecture straight to revision 3 without repeating the mistakes.

The skills follow the meta-rules (`skills/v8.1/prompt-writing`): standards as evaluative questions, real PROPER, HALF WAY, and COUNTER-EXAMPLE pairs from the Lecture 10 drafts, few bans, and the reasons behind each standard. The principle boxes stay owner-approved law: the skills copy them and answer findings around them.

## Group 1: The stand-alone summary edition (#2026_10_17_01_group_1)

`skills/v8.1/summary-edition/SKILL.md` and `examples.md`. The aim (short, visual, and readable alone; the stand-alone reader wins a conflict), a table from each map field to its place in the lesson, the skeleton (title page alone, the example, the components, the first division, the second division or sequence, one section per principle, putting the parts together, interview, where you will meet this, summary) and where each part of the full lecture moves, the opening on a worked example (the owner: "we need organic introduction of what is going on in the demo example, what is at stake"), the components behind the page, the shape of each idea (division, hierarchy, sequence, contrast, cause and effect, mapping; the owner: "we need to follow a division tree and then apply each branch"), the nine-step principle section, conditions in every description (the owner: "you need to repeat in summary form the various nuances and conditions"), stakes, facts before use, one consistent running example, verbatim principle boxes, and closing recaps that keep every nuance. The casebook holds twelve real cases from the Lecture 10 drafts.

## Group 2: Idea shapes and the figure blocks (#2026_10_17_01_group_2)

`skills/v8.1/summary-figures/SKILL.md` Sections 01 to 04 and `blocks.md`. Which block draws which shape, what every figure carries (its condition, what to look at, the lecture's real values), the hierarchy panel (the owner asked for a component tree on the left and the logic of the connection on the right) and its approved design after the owner reversed the first dark version, division and decision trees, and the full syntax of the revision 3 build.

## Group 3: Visual design and page flow (#2026_10_17_01_group_3)

`skills/v8.1/summary-figures/SKILL.md` Sections 05 and 06. The house style the owner chose (the `original` theme, `original-margins` with side margins of 22 mm, generous padding), the process for any new palette (start from the owner's references and a tested colour system, one neutral and one accent, green and red only as small signals, contrast measured at 4.5:1, a sample page before every lecture, a new theme file), the rejected themes as counter-examples with the owner's words ("this left-side border thickness is typical for modern AI slop"), and the page-flow rules.

## Review and alignment

Before the batch closed, a fresh reviewer checked the skills against the build code, the styles, `archive_lecture.py`, the map, the meta-rules, and the reference lesson, and reported 24 findings. The main ones, all addressed: Section 02 now says what may adapt from the map (the story) and what may not (the behaviour a proof requires), that code the lecture does not contain is run before it is printed, and that adapted proofs are reported; the reader is defined once, from the map's Terms, and the audit reuses it; a sequence axis gets a "you are here" map as a one-level tree, and a one-component lecture still gets a hierarchy panel; the visual standards are scoped to new palettes, with the house style's own bars and grey captions listed as the owner's choices; the lifecycle of a lesson (draft, accepted, archived) and the `--version` and `--reason` flags are written down; the stale README and file headers point to the skills. The reference lesson was brought in line: the map's proofs for the six logged lines, the four lines when the parent leaves the card out, the parse error of a ternary without `:`, and the same comment lines from both versions were added; the division heading and the Summary tree follow the two-level tree; the mini maps share the full tree's root; a `bad=` line was corrected; the comparison operators got a fact note.
